Honest answers to the questions that stop most first-time student
researchers from submitting.

## Do I need a lab or expensive equipment?

No. Several tracks, like [T3, Responsible AI, Ethics, and Policy](/tracks#T3),
are analysis and writing-based, not wet-lab-based. Track T2
covers computational and bioinformatics work as well as laboratory science,
so a laptop and a dataset can be enough for a real contribution.

## Do I need a professor or mentor?

The Call for Papers does not require one either way. [Individual
submissions are explicitly welcome](/call-for-papers), so you are eligible
to submit on your own. If a teacher or mentor is willing to advise you,
that is genuinely helpful, but it is not a requirement.

## What if my results are negative, or inconclusive?

That is normal science, not a failed project. The required paper sections
themselves say "results (**where applicable**)," and "limitations" is one
of the required sections too, because the review process expects you to discuss
what your research could not answer, not just what it could. Read more on
the [Submission](/submission) page.

## What if I'm the only person at my school doing this?

Both individual and team submissions are welcome, so you do not need a group
or a school club behind you to submit.

## What if my project fits more than one track?

That is fine. Pick whichever track your work leans on most. Reviewers with
relevant expertise are assigned based on the track you select, and they
judge the substance of your work, not which single label you chose. See
[Tracks](/tracks) for the full list.

## What if I've never written a research paper before?

Start with the [Submission](/submission) page, which breaks down exactly
what goes in a paper, section by section, with a visual example of what a
formatted page looks like. Every author submits into the same structure,
whether it is their first paper or their fifth.

## Can my team include students from different schools or countries?

Yes. There are no geographic restrictions on participation, and nothing
requires teammates to share a school. Each member's school (including
homeschools) just needs to be recognized by its own country's Ministry of
Education or equivalent government body — see
[Academic Integrity & Eligibility](/integrity) for the full statement.

## How much does it cost to submit or present?

No registration fee has been announced yet, and none will be collected
until one is decided and published. See [Registration](/registration) for
the fee tiers once they're set.

## What if my English isn't strong?

We will not pretend this does not matter. English is the conference's
working language, and "quality of writing and organization" is genuinely
one of the six criteria reviewers use, alongside things like scientific
merit and originality. It is one criterion among six, not a gate on its
own. Getting help proofreading, from a teacher, a friend, or a
translation tool, is a completely normal part of preparing a paper in a
second language.
